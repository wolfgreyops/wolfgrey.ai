03 — What you get

One page that does the whole job.

Not a brochure with a contact form buried at the bottom. The booking is the first thing on the page, because it's the only thing the visitor came for.

  • The urgent lane

    Left side, impossible to miss. Name, mobile, what's wrong — tapped in under thirty seconds on a phone, in a hallway, with one hand. It goes straight to you as a text, not into an inbox you check on Fridays.

  • The calendar lane

    Right side, for everything that isn't on fire — quotes, second opinions, annual service. They pick a day and a window. You set how many slots a day you'll take, so the calendar can never book you past what you can actually do.

  • You accept or you pass

    Nothing is automatically committed in your name. Every request arrives on your phone and waits for a yes or a no. Wrong side of town, wrong kind of job, week you're already buried — pass, and the customer hears back immediately instead of wondering.

  • Services, reviews, and the rest of it

    Below the booking: what you do, what people said, where you work, your license, how to reach you. Reviews pull from the places customers already left them. It's the whole site — the fold is just where it starts.

  • Built for the phone first

    Nearly everyone who needs you right now is standing in front of the problem holding a phone. Big targets, a call button that follows them down the page, and no pinching to read anything.

Talk it through

Straight answers

Questions people actually ask.

I already have a website. Does this replace it?

It can, or it can sit alongside it. Plenty of contractors keep an older site and point their ads and Google listing at this one, because this is the page built to convert someone who needs help today. If you'd rather replace the old one entirely, point your domain here and you're done.

What if I don't want the job that comes in?

Then you pass on it. Nothing is booked in your name without you saying yes. You see the job, the location and the customer's details on your phone, and you decide. The customer gets an answer either way, which is more than most of them get anywhere else.

Can it book me past what I can handle?

No. You set how many slots exist in a day, and days you don't work are simply closed. When a day fills up, it stops offering that day. The calendar can only ever offer what you've told it you have.

I'm not technical. Is this actually going to work for me?

You upload a logo, tap a color, and type your phone number. If you can post a photo of a finished job, you can do this. There's nothing to install, no plugins, no hosting to figure out, and nothing that breaks when you're not looking at it.

What about my existing reviews?

They pull in from where customers already left them, so you're not asking anybody to write a new one. Your rating shows up on the page underneath the booking, where it does the most good.

Who built this?

wolfgrey — a one-operator shop that builds AI and automation systems for small businesses. This started as a custom build for a plumbing contractor, worked well enough that other trades asked for it, and became a product instead of a project. Same template, same booking flow, now self-serve.
