Part 1

What is Claude?

Understanding AI assistants and how they can help you work smarter.

1Claude is an AI Assistant

Claude is an AI assistant built by Anthropic. Think of it as a knowledgeable collaborator that can help you write, analyze, research, code, and think through problems. It understands context, follows instructions, and can handle complex tasks across virtually any domain.

2It's a Conversation, Not a Search

Unlike Google, you don't type keywords. You have a conversation. Ask questions, provide context, and build on previous responses. Claude remembers everything in your current conversation, so you can say "make that shorter" or "explain that differently" without repeating yourself.

3Claude Doesn't "Know" Everything

Claude was trained on data with a knowledge cutoff, so it may not know about very recent events. It also can't browse the internet, access your files, or remember past conversations. Each new chat starts fresh. For current information, you'll need to provide context or use Claude's web search feature when available.

Getting Started

Head to claude.ai to start using Claude for free. Create an account and you're ready to go. The free tier has generous limits for most personal and small business use.

Know the Tools

Claude vs Claude Code vs Cowork

Three products, three different jobs. Pick the right one for how you work.

Claude — The Chat

The one most people know. Go to claude.ai, type a question, get an answer. It's a conversation — you go back and forth until you get what you need. Good for writing, brainstorming, analysis, learning, and quick tasks.

  • Best for: Writing emails, drafting proposals, summarizing documents, brainstorming, research
  • How it works: Web browser or mobile app. Type a message, get a response.
  • Feels like: Texting a really smart colleague
  • Cost: Free tier available. Pro plan $20/mo for more usage and features.

Claude Code — The Terminal

A command-line tool for developers and technical users. Runs in your terminal, can read and write files on your computer, execute commands, and build software. This is the tool wolfgrey uses to build automations and client projects.

  • Best for: Writing code, building automations, editing files in bulk, running scripts
  • How it works: Terminal/command line. You describe what to build, it writes and runs the code.
  • Feels like: Pair programming with a senior developer
  • Cost: Requires a Claude Pro ($20/mo) or Max ($100/mo) subscription. Also available via API.

Cowork — The Desktop Agent

The newest product. A desktop app (Mac and Windows) that gives Claude access to your actual files and folders. You point it at a folder, describe a task, and it works through it independently — organizing files, creating spreadsheets from scattered notes, generating reports. You don't babysit it; you check in when it's done.

  • Best for: File organization, document creation, data cleanup, multi-step office tasks
  • How it works: Desktop app. Grant access to a folder, describe the task, let it work.
  • Feels like: Delegating to a virtual assistant who can actually touch your files
  • Cost: Available on Max ($100/mo), Team, and Enterprise plans.

Part 2

The Art of Prompting

How to communicate with AI to get exactly what you need.

1Be Specific, Not Vague

The more specific your request, the better Claude's response. Vague prompts get generic answers. Specific prompts get useful answers.

Instead of... Try...
"Write about marketing" "Write a 200-word LinkedIn post about email marketing for B2B SaaS companies, focusing on subject line best practices"
"Help me with my email" "Rewrite this cold outreach email to be more conversational and under 100 words: [paste email]"
"Explain AI" "Explain AI to a small business owner who's never used it, focusing on practical applications for a 10-person consulting firm"

2Give Context

Claude can't read your mind. Share relevant background information to get tailored responses.

  • Who you are: "I run a 5-person marketing agency..."
  • What you need: "I need a follow-up email for a prospect who went silent..."
  • Constraints: "Keep it under 50 words, professional but warm tone..."
  • Examples: "Here's an email that worked well before: [paste example]"

3Specify Format

Tell Claude exactly how you want the output structured:

  • "Give me a bulleted list"
  • "Format as a table with columns for X, Y, and Z"
  • "Write this as an email with subject line"
  • "Create a step-by-step numbered guide"
  • "Keep it to 3 sentences"

Common Mistake

Don't treat Claude like a magic 8-ball with one-word questions. "Marketing?" won't get you anywhere. Full sentences with context will.

Part 3

The CRAFT Framework

A simple structure for writing effective prompts every time.

C — Context

Set the scene. Who are you? What's the situation? What background does Claude need?

"I'm a freelance web designer pitching to a local restaurant that's never had a professional website..."

R — Role

Tell Claude what role to play or what expertise to bring to the task.

"Act as an experienced sales consultant who specializes in small business clients..."

A — Action

Clearly state what you want Claude to do. Use action verbs.

"Write a proposal email that highlights the benefits of having a website for restaurant reservations..."

F — Format

Specify the output structure, length, and style you need.

"Format as a professional email, under 200 words, with a clear call-to-action at the end..."

T — Tone

Define the voice and energy of the response.

"Keep it friendly and conversational, not salesy. Confident but not pushy."

You Don't Need All 5

Not every prompt needs every element. Simple tasks might only need Action + Format. Complex tasks benefit from the full framework. Use what makes sense.

Part 4

Starter Prompts

Copy-paste these prompts to start using Claude immediately. Replace the [BRACKETS] with your info.

1

Explain Something Complex

Learning

Break down any concept so you actually understand it.

Explain [TOPIC] to me like I'm a smart professional who has no background in this area. Use analogies from everyday life. Start with the big picture, then get specific. No jargon unless you define it first.
2

Write a Professional Email

Writing

Draft emails faster with the right tone.

Write an email to [RECIPIENT/ROLE] about [TOPIC]. The goal is to [DESIRED OUTCOME]. Tone should be [professional/friendly/urgent/casual]. Keep it under [NUMBER] words. Include a clear next step or call-to-action.
3

Summarize Long Content

Analysis

Turn articles, reports, or documents into actionable summaries.

Summarize this [article/document/report] in 3-5 bullet points. Focus on: key findings, action items, and anything surprising or counterintuitive. Then give me one sentence on why this matters. [PASTE CONTENT HERE]
4

Brainstorm Ideas

Creative

Generate options when you're stuck.

I need ideas for [TOPIC/CHALLENGE]. Give me 10 options ranging from obvious/safe to creative/unconventional. For each idea, include one sentence on why it might work and one potential downside. My constraints are: [LIST ANY CONSTRAINTS].
5

Edit and Improve Writing

Writing

Make your writing clearer and more impactful.

Edit this text for clarity, conciseness, and impact. Fix any grammar issues. Make it sound [more professional/conversational/persuasive]. Keep my voice but make it stronger. Show me the edited version, then briefly explain the key changes you made. [PASTE YOUR TEXT HERE]
6

Create a Decision Framework

Strategy

Think through tough decisions systematically.

Help me decide between [OPTION A] and [OPTION B]. Create a comparison framework that considers: [LIST 3-5 FACTORS THAT MATTER TO YOU]. Weight each factor by importance. Then give me your recommendation with reasoning. What am I not considering?
7

Research a Topic

Research

Get up to speed quickly on any subject.

I need to understand [TOPIC] quickly for [PURPOSE]. Give me: 1. The 20% of knowledge that covers 80% of what I need to know 2. Key terminology I should understand 3. Common misconceptions to avoid 4. 3 follow-up questions I should explore Keep it practical and focused on application, not theory.
8

Create a Template

Systems

Build reusable templates for recurring tasks.

Create a reusable template for [TYPE OF DOCUMENT/PROCESS]. I do this [frequency] and it usually involves [KEY ELEMENTS]. Include placeholders I can fill in, with brief instructions for each section. Make it easy to copy-paste and customize quickly.
9

Prepare for a Meeting

Preparation

Walk in prepared with smart questions and talking points.

I have a meeting about [TOPIC] with [WHO]. The goal is to [DESIRED OUTCOME]. Help me prepare: 1. 5 smart questions to ask 2. Key points I should make 3. Potential objections and how to address them 4. A clear ask or next step to end with Context: [ANY RELEVANT BACKGROUND]
10

Debug a Problem

Problem Solving

Systematically troubleshoot issues.

I'm having this problem: [DESCRIBE ISSUE]. What I expected: [EXPECTED OUTCOME] What's actually happening: [ACTUAL OUTCOME] What I've already tried: [LIST ATTEMPTS] Help me figure out what's wrong. Give me a systematic troubleshooting checklist, starting with the most likely causes.
Part 5

More Starter Prompts

Five more prompts for common use cases.

11

Get Feedback on an Idea

Strategy

Pressure-test your ideas before committing.

Here's my idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA] Play devil's advocate. What could go wrong? What am I not seeing? Who would disagree and why? Then, if you were to improve this idea, what would you change? Be direct - I want honest feedback, not encouragement.
12

Create a Step-by-Step Process

Systems

Turn complex tasks into repeatable processes.

Create a step-by-step process for [TASK]. Assume I'm doing this for the first time. Include: numbered steps, time estimate for each step, common mistakes to avoid, and a checklist I can use to verify I did it right. Make it detailed enough that I could hand it off to someone else.
13

Rewrite for a Different Audience

Writing

Adapt content for different readers without starting over.

Rewrite this content for [NEW AUDIENCE]: [PASTE ORIGINAL CONTENT] The new audience: [DESCRIBE WHO THEY ARE, WHAT THEY CARE ABOUT, THEIR KNOWLEDGE LEVEL] Keep the core message but adjust vocabulary, examples, and framing to resonate with this specific audience.
14

Write Social Media Posts

Marketing

Generate engaging posts for any platform.

Write [NUMBER] social media posts for [PLATFORM] about [TOPIC]. My audience: [WHO FOLLOWS ME] My brand voice: [DESCRIBE YOUR STYLE] Goal: [ENGAGEMENT/TRAFFIC/AWARENESS/ETC] Include a mix of formats: question, tip, story, controversial take. Keep each post native to the platform's style and character limits.
15

Learn From Mistakes

Reflection

Turn failures into learning opportunities.

Something didn't go well: [DESCRIBE WHAT HAPPENED] Help me do a post-mortem: 1. What were the root causes? (Go deeper than surface-level) 2. What warning signs did I miss? 3. What would I do differently next time? 4. What process or system could prevent this in the future? 5. What's the silver lining or lesson here? Be honest but constructive.

Pro Tip: Iterate

Your first prompt rarely gives you exactly what you need. That's normal. Say "make it shorter," "add more examples," or "try a different approach." Claude learns from your feedback within the conversation. The magic is in the back-and-forth.

Part 6

Best Practices & Tips

Level up your Claude skills with these proven strategies.

Break Complex Tasks Into Steps

For big projects, don't try to do everything in one prompt. Break it down: "First, help me outline this article. Once we agree on structure, we'll write each section." This produces better results and gives you control over the direction.

Use Examples

If you have a style you like or a format you want, show Claude an example. "Here's a product description I like: [example]. Write 5 more in this style for these products: [list]." Examples are often better than lengthy explanations.

Ask Claude to Ask You Questions

Not sure what information Claude needs? Try: "I want to [goal]. Before we start, ask me any questions you need answered to do this well." Claude will identify gaps in the brief that you might have missed.

Request Multiple Options

Instead of asking for one answer, ask for 3-5 variations. "Give me 5 different subject lines for this email, ranging from conservative to bold." Then pick the best or combine elements.

Save Your Best Prompts

When a prompt works well, save it. Build a personal library of templates you can reuse. Over time, you'll have a toolkit that makes Claude even more powerful for your specific needs.

Things Claude Won't Do Well

  • Real-time information: Claude doesn't know today's stock prices or yesterday's news
  • Math with many steps: Complex calculations can have errors - verify important numbers
  • Personal memory: Each new conversation starts fresh - Claude won't remember last week's chat
  • Truly random: If you need genuine randomness, use a proper random generator
  • Perfect accuracy: Always verify facts for critical decisions, especially dates and statistics
