The CRAFT Prompting Framework

Stop writing vague prompts that get vague results. CRAFT gives you a repeatable system for getting exactly what you need from any AI tool.

C

Context

Give the AI background information it needs to understand your situation. The more relevant context, the better the output.

Example: "I run a 10-person marketing agency focused on B2B SaaS companies. We've been in business for 3 years and are trying to expand into enterprise clients."
R

Role

Tell the AI who to be. A specific role shapes how it thinks, what expertise it draws on, and how it communicates.

Example: "Act as a senior sales strategist who has helped 50+ agencies break into enterprise sales. You're direct, practical, and focused on actionable advice."
A

Action

Be specific about what you want done. Vague requests get vague results. Tell the AI exactly what task to perform.

Example: "Create a cold outreach email sequence (3 emails) for reaching enterprise marketing directors. Each email should be under 150 words."
F

Format

Define how you want the output structured. Tables, bullet points, numbered lists, specific sections—be explicit.

Example: "Format each email with: Subject line, Body text, Suggested send timing, Follow-up notes. Use markdown formatting."
T

Tone

Set the voice and style. Professional? Casual? Technical? The tone shapes whether the output sounds like you.

Example: "Write in a confident but not arrogant tone. Sound like a peer, not a salesperson. Avoid jargon and buzzwords."

Common Prompting Mistakes

Most prompts fail before the AI even starts. Here are three patterns we see constantly — and how applying the CRAFT framework fixes each one.

Before Vague Request

Write me a marketing email for my product.

After CRAFT-Enhanced

You are a senior email copywriter for DTC brands. Write a 150-word product launch email for our new organic dog treats targeting millennial pet owners. Use a friendly, casual tone. Include a subject line, 3 benefit bullets, and a single CTA button. Format as plain text with clear section breaks.

Before Missing Context

Help me respond to this customer complaint.

After Context-Rich

You are a customer success manager at a 12-person SaaS company. A long-term client (2+ years, $400/mo plan) emailed saying our latest update broke their team's workflow and they're considering canceling. Draft a response that acknowledges the issue, offers a concrete fix timeline, and includes a retention incentive. Keep it under 200 words, empathetic but professional.

Before No Format Spec

Analyze our Q4 sales data and give me insights.

After Format-Specified

You are a revenue analyst. Review the attached Q4 sales CSV for a B2B cleaning supply company. Produce a report with: (1) Executive summary — 3 sentences max, (2) Top 5 trends as a numbered list with percentage changes, (3) Three risks formatted as a table with columns: Risk, Likelihood, Impact, (4) Recommended next steps as a bullet list. Use plain language — this goes to the owner, not the finance team.

Prompt Chaining

The real power of prompting isn't a single shot — it's building a chain. Each prompt feeds its output into the next, turning raw information into a finished deliverable in steps. Think of it like an assembly line for thinking.

1

Analyze Raw Feedback

Prompt: Here are 47 customer support tickets from the last 30 days. Categorize each into themes (e.g., billing, onboarding, feature request, bug). For each category, note the frequency and average sentiment. Output as a structured table.

Output Categorized insights with frequency + sentiment scores
2

Prioritize Action Items

Prompt: Using the categorized feedback below, identify the top 5 issues by business impact. For each, suggest a concrete fix. Score each on effort (1-5) vs. impact (1-5) and rank by best ROI. Format as a prioritized action list with owner suggestions for a 10-person team.

Output Ranked action items with effort/impact scores
3

Build Implementation Timeline

Prompt: Take the prioritized action items below and create a 6-week implementation plan. Week 1-2: quick wins (effort ≤ 2). Week 3-4: medium projects. Week 5-6: larger initiatives. Include milestones, dependencies, and a "done" definition for each item. Format as a week-by-week table I can paste into a project tracker.

Output Ready-to-use 6-week project plan
⚡

47 raw tickets became a 6-week action plan in 3 prompts. Each step produces clean, structured output that the next step can build on. That's the multiplier effect of chaining — you're not just asking questions, you're building a pipeline.
